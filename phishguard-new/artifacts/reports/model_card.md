# Model card - PhishGuard

**Adversarially Robust Phishing Defense using Email, URL and Behavioral Features** (capstone BAI-17)

## Model details

- **Version**: `pg-0e0dffa24f`
- **Trained**: 2026-09-22T08:49:03Z
- **Type**: calibrated logistic fusion over 4 heterogeneous members
- **Members**: rules, engineered, charngram, wordtfidf
- **Feature families**: email, url, behavioral
- **Engineered features**: 265
- **Calibration**: isotonic
- **Active defences**: D-NORM, D-URL, D-ADVTRAIN, D-ENSEMBLE, D-ABSTAIN, D-VELOCITY
- **Built with**: Python 3.11.15, scikit-learn 1.8.0, numpy 2.4.4

## Intended use

Assessing inbound email at a mail gateway or in a SOC triage queue, producing a calibrated phishing probability, a three-way decision band and an evidence list an analyst can act on.

**In scope**: organisational inbound email where header, URL and sender-relationship context are available.

**Out of scope**, and not safe to use for:

- Deciding to block mail with no human review path. The REVIEW band exists because the model is not the last word.
- Attributing an attack to a person or organisation.
- Any use where a missed detection carries irreversible harm without a compensating control.
- Languages and mail cultures absent from the training corpus; performance there is unmeasured, not merely lower.

## Training data

- **Corpus**: synthetic(n=9000, seed=20260907)
- **Split strategy**: grouped-stratified (campaign-disjoint, near-duplicate scrubbed)
- **Training messages**: 4025 for members, 1492 for fusion, 1233 for calibration
- **Adversarial augmentation**: +1747 perturbed phishing examples

## Threat model

The adversary is a competent phishing operator with full control of the message they send and black-box access to the verdict, under a query budget. They cannot fabricate the recipient's correspondence history, age their own domain retroactively, or send from the brand's genuine domain.

| ID | Attack family | Attacker capability | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-LEX | Lexical and semantic perturbation | Free - the attacker authors the text | Low |
| A-UNI | Unicode and homoglyph obfuscation | Free - any mail client renders these identically | Low |
| A-URL | URL obfuscation and redirection | Cheap - requires only a redirect host or a shortener account | Low to medium |
| A-STRUCT | Structural and HTML manipulation | Free - the attacker authors the HTML part | Low |
| A-HDR | Header and identity shaping | Cheap for the attacker's own domain; impossible for the brand's real domain, which is domain compromise and out of scope | Medium |
| A-BEH | Behavioral and timing mimicry | Attacker controls send time, pacing and framing; cannot fabricate the recipient's prior correspondence history or the age of their own domain | High - low-and-slow sending cuts campaign reach |
| A-TRUST | Payload relocation onto reputable infrastructure | Requires an account on the hosting platform and survives only until abuse takedown; the redirector must already exist on the trusted domain | Medium - accounts are cheap, takedowns are fast |
| A-CHANNEL | Out-of-band payload delivery | Requires a staffed phone line or an image pipeline, and converts far fewer victims per message because the victim must act manually | High - callback fraud needs live operators |

### Explicitly out of scope

| ID | Threat | Why out of scope | Handled by |
|---|---|---|---|
| OOS-1 | Compromise of a genuine brand or partner domain | Mail genuinely originating from the real domain passes every header and reputation check by construction. This is an authentication and account-security problem, not a content-classification one. | MFA on mail accounts, DMARC enforcement, egress monitoring |
| OOS-2 | White-box gradient-based evasion | Assumes the attacker holds model weights. If weights leak, the correct response is rotation and retraining, not a robustness claim. | Artefact access control, model registry auditing |
| OOS-3 | Training-data poisoning through the analyst feedback loop | Feedback is stored and surfaced for review but is never used to retrain automatically. Retraining is a deliberate, reviewed action. | Human review of feedback before any retraining run |
| OOS-4 | Malicious payload analysis (attachment detonation, page fetching) | The system deliberately never resolves a URL or opens an attachment. Fetching attacker-controlled content from the gateway would create an SSRF surface and leak victim telemetry to the attacker. | A dedicated sandbox detonation service downstream |

## Defensive controls

| ID | Control | Cost | Residual gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-NORM | Text canonicalization | ~0.15 ms/message; no measurable clean-accuracy loss | Confusables outside the curated table, and paraphrase attacks that use no obfuscation at all, pass through unchanged. |
| D-URL | URL canonicalization | ~0.1 ms/URL; strictly offline, no DNS or HTTP lookups | A shortener whose target is not embedded in the query string cannot be resolved offline; the wrapper itself remains a feature. |
| D-ADVTRAIN | Adversarial training | training set grows by the augmentation multiplier; inference unchanged | Generalizes to unseen transforms only to the extent that they resemble the training perturbations; a genuinely novel attack family is out of scope. |
| D-ENSEMBLE | Multi-view fusion | ~4x single-model inference; still sub-10 ms/message on CPU | Members share the text channel, so a strong semantic paraphrase moves them together. Behavioral features are the decorrelating leg. |
| D-ABSTAIN | Confidence banding / abstention | analyst time proportional to the review-band width | An attack that pushes a message all the way below the review threshold still evades silently; banding converts some evasions into review load, not zero. |
| D-VELOCITY | Campaign-burst guard | one bounded arithmetic adjustment; no extra model call | Low-and-slow, individually targeted spear-phishing produces no burst and is unaffected. |

## Ethical considerations and privacy

- **Data minimisation.** No message content is stored. The audit trail holds a salted digest of the subject, a pseudonymised sender identifier, the sender's domain, sizes, the verdict and the evidence titles.
- **No content in logs.** Structured logs carry request ids, scores and domains; the logging layer drops any field that could hold content.
- **No outbound fetching.** URLs are never resolved and attachments are never opened, so the system cannot leak victim telemetry to an attacker or be turned into an SSRF primitive.
- **Human oversight is structural.** The REVIEW band routes uncertain messages to an analyst; feedback is recorded but never retrains the model automatically.
- **Asymmetric harms.** A false positive delays legitimate business communication; a false negative may cost credentials or money. The operating point is chosen on a false-positive budget for that reason, and both numbers are reported rather than averaged away.
- **Named-entity risk.** The brand and domain lists are heuristics. A legitimate organisation whose domain resembles a brand's can be penalised; the REVIEW band and the analyst feedback loop exist to catch that, and the lists are reviewable in `src/phishguard/features/lexicons.py`.

## Limitations

1. The corpus is synthetic. It is campaign-structured, difficulty-balanced and leakage-audited, but it is a model of phishing rather than a sample of it; comparative results transfer more reliably than absolute ones.
2. Robustness is measured against this suite of 35 transforms in 8 families. A genuinely novel attack family is by definition untested.
3. Behavioural features assume a mail gateway. Without them the system degrades to email and URL evidence and records that it did.
4. Calibration is measured in-distribution. Under drift the probabilities move, which is why `/api/v1/stats` tracks the score distribution over time.
5. The system detects; it does not remediate. Quarantine, user notification and takedown are downstream responsibilities.

## Maintenance

- **Retrain** when the analyst-disagreement rate reported by `/api/v1/stats` rises materially, or when the score distribution shifts.
- **Re-run the adversarial suite** on every model change; the acceptance gates block a release whose robustness regressed.
- **Review the brand and TLD lists** quarterly; they are the fastest-ageing part of the system.
- Regenerate this card with `phishguard card` after every training run.
